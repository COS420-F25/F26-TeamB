## Mission Statement:
Empowering users to build effective AI habits aligned with long-term goals.
## Proposed Problem Statements:
### Problem Statement 1:
People are increasingly using and relying on AI for various purposes (coding, writing assistance, fact checking, managing daily tasks, lifestyle advice, etc.), and they often don’t fully understand the implications of that usage, or think about the way their usage affects themselves and/or others. When people reflect on their AI use they can gain a deeper understanding of beneficial and non-beneficial ways to use AI and understand the impacts of their actions, but it is often hard for people to remember what they've actually used AI for and the context of that use & non-use. For example, when a student uses AI excessively for completing HW assignments they may lose their ability to do that type of work on their own. If they never reflect on their usage they will remain reliant. There are some existing agents out there that help you reflect on AI usage, such as Slime, an AI companion that reflects on your usage in real time or Claude Reflect that creates a summary of how you’ve used Claude over an extended period of time. Slime can be used with multiple AIs, but doesn’t summarize long term patterns, it builds short term patterns over single conversations. While Claude uses long term patterns, it reflects only on your Claude usage and does not help you reflect on your use of other platforms. How can we design an AI agent that can help users reflect on short and long term AI usage patterns across all AI platforms to help them think about and refine their AI use?
### Problem Statement 2:
People use AI, but the regular use of generative AI can present false information and can potentially alter information without the user knowing. When people reflect on their AI use they can somewhat notice these issues, but AI frequently presents information confidently, even if it is wrong. To fact check AI’s output, you may need to research yourself and may need to possess knowledge beforehand , but not everyone either takes the time to do this or is aware that AI is this flawed. If that knowledge and/or information were more accessible via either curated data for reflection of their AI use or a platform to balance AI content vs traditional content, and AI could assist people in reflecting where the people still make the final judgments, then people's AI use could be better and more factual. How might we help people use AI better in a way that doesn't cause errors, is more collaborative, and helps people notice the flaws and issues in their data, including when to not use or pause using AI?
### Problem Statement 3:
People use AI, but non-reflective AI use can lead to dependency and a lack of active learning. When people use AI with the intention of learning and reflecting, they can somewhat notice effects, but it's hard to remember over the long term in what context they've actually used AI for. To reflect, then have ideas to improve AI use, that is better when you have expertise, but to gain expertise in using AI requires reading a lot of material and knowing things. If that expertise were more accessible via tools like Claude Reflect, a tool that tracks and reviews AI usage, and AI could assist people in reflecting but keep it so the people still make the final judgments, people's AI use could be changed to a learning tool. How might we help people use AI better in a way that doesn't totally cause dependence, is more collaborative, and helps people notice what could be changed for better, including when to be more reflective about AI use?
