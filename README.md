## Mission Statement:
Empowering users to build effective AI habits aligned with long-term goals.
## Problem Statement:
AI makes errors, but it is hard to notice when you did not catch / correct it in its output. As generative AI continues to grow competent, it’s harder to spot errors in its output. One may not even know the errors in order to find them. Though we don’t always know what is an error. This proves even more true if it’s assumed the specific agent is correct. However, if users know the agent made an error, the user would have addressed it. If an AI tool inserted errors on purpose into its output, and asked users to find them before using the output then they would have a basis in regards to their ability to catch and fix errors for reflecting on and improving that skill. This would not only lead users to take more caution, but also provide a deeper understanding and recognition of AI and its patterns. Users need expertise about what is good use of AI: To not only understand its imperfection of causing errors but understand the information they are being given. 

Reflecting on your agentic use can help improve your ability to use it, however, it's hard to remember how you're actually using them over time, the context of use, and if you are improving your ability to identify your usage of AI as a tool. This is because the human memory is limited and cannot recall specifics that well and is inconsistent. Computers have great recall and augment memory via search and the processing of past AI prompts and retain usage logs. 

Building expertise takes time, experimentation, and reflection, and to build that expertise you need to do the first two things, which are already hard. Somewhere in the world, there are people who have taken the time and effort to gain expertise, created documents, recommendations, and research on how to use generative AI more effectively or may abstain on its use at all, depending. If users had access to research-based or expert advice and practices around using it, they might learn from such resources and obtain more knowledge. How might we help people use generative AI better in a way that helps them notice the flaws and issues in their agentic interactions – by adding known errors, doesn't cause ignorant dependence, is more collaborative, helps people notice what could be changed for the better (via expert advice/resources), and actually become more reflective by improving their agentic use?
